What is a Computer Network?

A computer network is a collection of interconnected computing devices that exchange data and share resources with one another. These network devices use a system of rules, called communications protocols, to transmit information over physical infrastructure (such as Ethernet cables and fiber optics) or wireless technologies (such as Wi-Fi and cellular networks).

Networks are broadly categorized based on their geographic scale:

Local Area Network (LAN): Connects computers and devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, school, or office building. LANs offer high-speed data transfer rates and low latency.

Wide Area Network (WAN): Spans large geographic areas, connecting multiple LANs together across cities, countries, or globally. The Internet is the largest and most prominent example of a Wide Area Network.

What is a Wireless Router? (Wi-Fi Router)

A wireless router is a hardware device that acts as the central gateway connecting local network devices to the broader internet while establishing a Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN). In modern networking, a consumer router combines the functionality of multiple standalone devices into one unit: a router, a network switch, and a wireless access point (WAP).

When internet data packets arrive from an Internet Service Provider (ISP), the router determines the correct destination device on the local network and forwards the data accordingly. It broadcasts wireless signals across standardized frequency bands—most commonly 2.4 GHz (which provides broader coverage through obstacles) and 5 GHz or 6 GHz (which provide significantly higher bandwidth over shorter distances).

Beyond data routing, wireless routers implement essential security mechanisms. They enforce Network Address Translation (NAT) to allow dozens of local devices to share a single public IP address, run built-in firewalls to block unauthorized incoming connections, and use encryption protocols such as WPA2 or WPA3 to secure wireless traffic from eavesdropping.

What is an IP Address?

An Internet Protocol (IP) address is a unique numerical or alphanumeric identifier assigned to every device connected to a computer network. Operating at the network layer of the OSI model, an IP address fulfills two main purposes: identifying the host/network interface and providing location addressing to route data packets across networks.

IP addresses exist in two standard versions:

IPv4 (Internet Protocol Version 4): Uses a 32-bit integer structure expressed in dot-decimal notation (e.g., 192.168.1.1). IPv4 supports approximately 4.3 billion unique addresses globally. Due to the exponential proliferation of internet-connected devices, IPv4 address space has become exhausted.
